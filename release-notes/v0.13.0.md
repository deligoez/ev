Themes read from contents: places with things in them and no theme now show what a theme could
be written from, in `ev themes` and in `ev ui`.

## New

- **`ev themes [<place>]`.** Every container or furniture that holds things but has no theme,
  most things first, with the words its contents share (each counted once per thing, rarer
  words first, written as the inventory writes them; colours and numbers left out), a sample of
  the contents, and the themed place those words read most like. Kits recorded as items are left
  out: they are named for what they are.
- **In `ev ui` too.** A themeless place's details end in "No theme yet", with the same words and
  the place it reads like, so a theme can be written while looking at it.
- **The skill asks for a theme on every toured place**, written in the person's words from this
  evidence and recorded once they agree.

## Why

A theme is the summary every placement answer leans on, the way a tree index's node summaries
steer its search. Measured against a reviewed answer key, most placement misses were places with
no theme or a shared one, not word matching. On a copy of the author's inventory, theming the
four themeless places put three more things in their best place (253 → 256 of 410), and
hand-written themes raised the right place's score on sample queries (an antenna cable 29 → 45).
