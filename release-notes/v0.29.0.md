The purchase list after a decade of receipts, and the rough edges left from 0.28.0. Lines that
are never a thing get their bucket set by hand and keep it through a re-import, who a line was
billed to can be listed and searched, and the purchase figures count only the household's own
spending. The last rough edges are gone: several moves made at once, a long `ev todo` read a
part at a time, exit codes that mean one thing each, undoing that puts parts back together,
sketches kept inside their holder, and text that names places and purchases by name. No schema
change.

## What the purchases needed this week

Importing a decade of receipts showed where the purchase list was hard to work with.

- **A line's bucket can be set by hand:** `ev buy bucket <line> durable|clothing|digital|service`,
  for a line entered by hand or from a source that cannot tell. A line linked to a thing is not
  made digital or a service while linked. A re-import that gives no `bucket` keeps the one a
  line has, instead of setting it back to `durable`.
- **Who a line was billed to** (an Apple ID of a family member) is in `ev buy list`, found by
  `--query`, and filters the list with `--billed-to <account>`.
- **The purchase figures in `ev stats`** count the household's own spending: a line dismissed as
  not ours, a duplicate, returned, or kept elsewhere is left out of the years, shops and buckets.
  One used up or given away still counts: it was bought and paid for.
- **An import names the fields it did not read** (`unknown_fields`), so a misspelt field
  (`orderd_at`) no longer loses its value without a word. A currency is a code in use (ISO 4217):
  a typo such as `EUO` is refused, naming the line.
- **Why a line could be a thing** comes with its kind and what it names, and reads in the
  reader's language.
- **`ev todo` and stats agree** on what a thing is worth: its linked lines together.

## Rough edges taken off

The rough edges left from the past-belongings release, each fixed and held by a test.

- **Several at once:** `ev done` and `ev cancel` take several records, all or none.
- **A long `ev todo` read a part at a time:** `ev todo --only tasks,uncounted` (and `only` on the
  MCP `todo` tool) lists those sections, with the counts of all; an answer too long for MCP says
  how to narrow it.
- **Exit codes mean one thing each:** 2 is a request written wrong (or about the wrong kind of
  record), 5 one the record's state does not allow: a line not linked, a kit named twice, a cell
  outside the grid, more units than are left.
- **Undoing:** a part of a thing that was set aside and kept, or recorded gone by mistake, joins
  the rest beside it again; a leaving taken back takes back the reason `--why` added to the note.
  A part split off a record carries its origin in its history, not in an English note.
- **A swap with a past thing** can be given in a batch line (`traded_for`) and is written with it,
  all or none. A batch line with both `gone` and `of` says they do not go together.
- **Saying again what is already so** is refused: a place already toured (unless what it holds
  changed since) or kept, a thing already lost, or lent again to whom it is with.
- **Sketches:** measures are kept to the millimetre, furniture and boxes cannot lie outside a
  holder of known size (a room may: a balcony), and a place reads as `x 120 · y 40 cm`.
- **Finding:** a typo of a whole word ranks above one that only matches the start of another, and
  `ev suggest` no longer offers a holder that is leaving or lost as a place.
- **Text:** history names places and purchase lines by name, and says where a lost thing was
  found rather than that it moved; what an unlink leaves comes with a command that runs as it is;
  an asking price shows its currency; the past lists show how many there were and what came for
  a swap; `ev progress` lists places under their holder and names each task once; a count of one
  is not written.
